## Overall Impression

What did not hold up is the interior. The conviction that produced the specimen sheet did not survive the trip to the Operate pages: cards.html opens with a 320px form and the card starts at y=605 on mobile; game.html's sticky panel covers 15 of 48 tiles at 390px while telling you to "choose any tile." That is the honest headline.

## What's Working

**The homepage is the product, not a pitch for it.** The plate is driven by the real dictionary, so what a visitor learns on the homepage is exactly what they get in chapter 1. Swap in any other product and the composition collapses, because the composition *is* the interaction.

**The failure states are more honest than the success states.** Speech distinguishes "no API" from "no German voice." Flashcards write a different empty message per cause. The drawer's zero-state disables both review buttons rather than letting you launch an empty deck. Most products this size ship one generic "Nothing here yet."

**Ink and geometry are disciplined enough to be a real system.** One gradient in 539 lines and it is a paper fold. Hard zero-blur offsets instead of soft shadows. Serif reserved for German, tracked sans for machinery. The rules mostly hold — and where they break, they break in exactly two places, named below.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Storage-blocked browsers get a blank reader.** `app.js:36-38` — three unguarded `localStorage.getItem()` calls at module scope. Where storage access throws (Safari with cookies blocked, partitioned/embedded contexts, strict privacy modes) the whole `app.js` IIFE aborts, `window.GermanReader` is never assigned, and reader.js, cards.js and game.js all fail. Reproduced in an opaque-origin iframe: read.html rendered nav, title, "0 of 90 words reached", an empty TOC, footer — **and zero chapters**. The `data-storage-notice` that exists for exactly this stayed hidden, because the code that shows it never ran. The product's pitch is "no account, nothing to install"; the one browser configuration that makes that pitch attractive is the one that breaks it, silently. *Fix:* wrap the three reads in the try/catch pattern store.js already uses, and set the storage flag so the existing notice fires. → `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] "It will come back in your cards" is a promise the product cannot keep.** `game.js:78` says it; `cards.js:45-47` supports only `custom | starred | seen` — there is **no `learning` source**. Verified: after a board with 17 words marked still-learning, `WORTWEG.learning().length === 17`, `wortweg-custom-set === null`, and the finish CTA is a bare `cards.html` that deals 24 random words from all 244. This breaks the exact loop the homepage sells. It is a regression from this session: I added the learning state without adding a way to study it. *Fix:* add a `learning` source chip, and have `checkFinished()` write the review set and point the CTA at `cards.html?custom=1`, reusing `launchReview` verbatim. → `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] The board can hard-lock on a device with no German voice.** `game.js:109` registers `Reveal + hear` as an audio control. If `syncAudioControls()` runs while a tile is selected, Reveal is disabled — and so are "I knew it" and "Still learning", which only enable after a reveal. Reproduced: `deadEnd: true`. Chrome fires `voiceschanged` more than once, so this is not theoretical. Also mine, from wiring the audio-availability system. *Fix:* unregister Reveal; keep `speak()` in the handler (it already returns false harmlessly) and relabel to "Reveal" when speech is unavailable. → `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Progress and the "Tapped in a story" deck are earned by scrolling.** `reader.js:206` calls `markSeen()` for every segment an IntersectionObserver passes. Scrolling read.html 4000px in ~2 seconds with zero taps produced 11 seen words, "11 of 90 words reached", and three chapters carrying a green completion check nobody read. cards.html then offers that set labelled **"Tapped in a story"**, with an empty message reading "You have not tapped any words in a story yet." *Fix:* either relabel to "Reached while reading" (honest, shippable today) or gate `markSeen` on an actual tap and keep the observer for position only. The second is the better product. → `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] On cards.html and game.html the object is below the fold or behind a panel.** `deck-settings` measures 320px at 390px wide, pushing the flashcard to y=605 with the answer row off-screen; the panel holds 7 chips a learner touches once. On the board, rectangle-intersection shows the sticky quiz panel covering **15 of 48 tiles** at 390px — 283px of a 650px viewport, permanently, while saying "Choose any tile to begin." *Fix:* collapse deck settings into a one-line `<details>` summary; make the board a scrolling region above a docked panel, or shrink the panel to word + Reveal until after the reveal. → `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] Green and ochre each carry two contradictory meanings, in one viewport.** `.level-stamp` is green (`styles.css:367`) and `.tile.known` is green (`styles.css:430`), so on index.html a single screen shows a green A1 level stamp, a green "known" tile, and a green swatch captioned "known". Green means both "this word is A1" and "you know this word." Separately, `.tile:hover/.current` is solid ochre and `.tile.learning` is ochre-inset. Your own stated system says ochre is attention and green is confirmed knowledge; the level stamp violates it 90 times over. *Fix:* strip color from `.level-stamp` — make it `var(--muted)` with its existing `1px solid currentColor` box; it is metadata, not state. Give `.tile.current` an ink inset ring instead of an ochre fill. → `/impeccable colorize`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer).** The nav is `Lesen · Wortfeld · Karteikarten` — three untranslated German words, with the English glosses only in `aria-label`, invisible to a sighted beginner. He taps `Berlin` — the most obvious proper noun on the homepage — and nothing happens at all, because it is not a dictionary entry and the hint just told him "Every underlined word is live. Tap one." The plate is pre-filled on load, so his first tap may read as "nothing changed." On a 390×844 phone, `Begin chapter one` sits at y=913, below the fold.

**Casey (distracted mobile).** cards.html: 320px of chips, then 45px of card. game.html: the panel covers 15 tiles and she must scroll a 1185px board to find one that isn't behind it. read.html: first German sentence at y=801. The narrowest word tap target measured **23px** (`in`) — under the 24px minimum, and German is full of two-letter words.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent).** Flipping a flashcard is silent: `role="button" aria-label="Reveal flashcard"` with no `aria-live`, and after the flip the accessible name is *still* "Reveal flashcard". The hint never clears either — the flipped card still reads "Click to reveal · or press space". `EN Translations` has no initial `aria-pressed` (it is added on first click), so a screen reader cannot tell it is a toggle. Word interactivity is signalled by a 1px dotted `--line` underline at ~1.4–1.6:1 against the page, far under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 requires for a non-text indicator. Credit where due: the drawer does real focus trapping with `inert`, restores focus intelligently, and yields Escape to the popover first.

**Maya, 34, six weeks into A1, twenty minutes on the train.** She cannot find the second story — `intermediate.html` is linked from exactly one place on the site, the footer of read.html. She cannot study what she got wrong. She cannot stop at a chapter: all 30 render into one 34,800px page with a single ending at the bottom, so a 30-chapter course spends thirty natural moments of closure and uses none of them. And the inflection bridge is missing: she taps `große` and gets `groß`, taps `heiße` and gets `heißen`, and the plate never says which form she tapped or that it is inflected — which is precisely the hardest thing about early German. The data supports it (`entry.f` holds the forms); the UI throws it away.

## Minor Observations

- `.icon-button` is a 50% circle (`styles.css:111`) in a system that declares square corners — and exactly one of them, the drawer close, is overridden back to 0. Pick one.
- The flashcard denominator grows as you fail (`1 / 24` → `2 / 25`). Correct behavior, demoralising display. Consider `2 / 24 · 1 repeating`.
- The board's color key exists on the homepage but not on the board; game.html explains it in prose using the word "ochre", which most English speakers cannot map to a color. Move the swatch legend onto the board.
- `data-chapter-count` is queried in `reader.js:150` and matches nothing in any HTML file. Dead selector.
- `GERMAN_STORIES.beginner` in german-data.js is never loaded by any page. Dead content.
- `license.html` is the only page without `.footer-links`, so its footer alignment differs.
- `og-image-v2.png` is 1.05 MB for a 1200×630 asset on a site whose pitch is "nothing to install."
- After marking a tile the panel keeps showing the word with every control disabled; it should return to "Wähle ein Wort." or keep the meaning visible.

## Questions to Consider

1. If progress is granted by scrolling, what is the number 90 actually measuring? Right now it means "scrolled past" and claims "read."
2. Why does a 30-chapter course have exactly one ending? What changes if read.html takes a `?kapitel=` parameter and each chapter closes with its own summary and a "Kapitel 04 →"?
3. Four word states, zero explanation — is `starred` doing anything the other three don't? Would three be clearer? Would two?
4. The plate shows `groß` when you tapped `große`. Is the lemma the answer, or is the bridge the answer? You have the forms data and show the destination without the road.
5. The homepage argues at length, in a code comment, that it refuses the pitch pattern — so why does cards.html open with a form and game.html open with a stat strip? The homepage earns its thesis; the interior pages have not been held to it.
