## Design Specificity Verdict

**Substantially authored, wearing a stock skeleton.** Your instinct is picking up real signal — but it is misattributed. The *visual system* is one of the more genuinely committed I've reviewed. The *page skeleton* and about eight specific artifacts are what smell.

**LLM assessment.** The Berlin Print Workshop is really shipped, not costumed: 0 radius throughout, 1px rules doing structural work (`.path-grid` and `.word-board` are literal ruled grids built from borders, not gaps), a disciplined 4/5/8/10/12px hard-offset ladder, and blur used exactly twice — on the popover and the drawer, the only two things that genuinely float. Each ink keeps one job across all six pages, including license.html. `styles.css:43` puts a print-sheet centre fold at 2.5% opacity. Six selectors carry `hyphens: auto` + `overflow-wrap: anywhere` specifically so `Bahnhofshalle` doesn't break the grid. Nobody adds those from a template.

But index.html's *frame* is the canonical landing skeleton in canonical order: nav → hero (copy left / poster right) → 3-up metric band → eyebrow + heading + 3 feature cards → quote band → footer. The suit is beautifully made; the mannequin's pose is stock. read.html, cards.html and game.html are the opposite — their composition is genuinely product-specific and not interchangeable with anything.

## Overall Impression

You have built a genuinely good design system and then buried the product under it. The Berlin Print Workshop is real, load-bearing, and specific — the `.tile` state ladder communicates learning state through nothing but flat ink, which is exactly why a 48-tile board stays readable at a glance. That constraint is doing work.

What's wrong is not slop in the visual language. It's that **the interface explains itself instead of starting.** On a 390px phone the first German sentence sits at y=1572 — 1.9 screens of syllabus before the thing the user came for. And the highest-frequency emotional beat in the entire product, "I don't know this word," ends in a panel that cannot be closed on touch.

The single biggest opportunity: delete the explaining. Everything above the first learning segment on read.html is the product apologizing for itself.

## What's Working

**The focus-card → sentence pairing** (`reader.js:95-114`, `styles.css:249-270`). It eliminates the recall gap: the word appears with article, plural, level and gloss, then immediately as the only vermilion-underlined token in a sentence built around it. The answer stays on screen while you read the question, so a learner is never punished for forgetting something they met four seconds ago. The 90-segment structure guarantees the interface never introduces two new things at once.

**The interface teaches German through its own chrome.** `Wörter`, `Kapitel 01`, `Neues Wort`, `Deine Sammlung`, `Geschafft.` — always in positions where context disambiguates. It converts navigation overhead, normally dead time, into low-stakes exposure. It is the single most category-specific decision in the codebase, and no template produces it.

**The print system is load-bearing, not decorative.** Blur appears exactly twice, on the only two floating things. Every ink keeps one semantic job across six pages. The 1px rules build the grids rather than decorating them.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The word popover cannot be dismissed on touch.**
`app.js:63-98`. Zero close buttons in `.pop-actions`, no outside-click handler, Escape-only (`app.js:97`) — and Escape doesn't exist on a phone. At 390px the panel is 360px wide, 92% of the viewport, fixed over the reading column.
*Why it matters:* a learner's first act of curiosity permanently occludes the page for the rest of the session. This is the most-used interaction in the product.
*Fix:* add a close control to `.pop-actions` (`app.js:82-86`) and a document-level click handler calling `closePopover()` when the target is outside the popover and not a `.word`. The `event.stopPropagation()` at `reader.js:25` exists for exactly that handler and is currently dead code.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] read.html buries the story under a screen and a half of chrome.**
`read.html:33-58`. First German sentence at y=1572 on mobile, y≈1154 on desktop. The promise was "Read your way into German"; the delivery is a syllabus. Two of the four stat cells are constants rendered in the loudest type on the page.
*Fix:* delete `.course-intro` (`read.html:36-39`), cut `.course-stats` to two cells, move the tool pills and TOC below the first segment. Target the first German sentence above 500px on mobile.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable distill`

**[P1] Keyboard focus is undesigned, and the closed drawer is a tab trap.**
`styles.css` contains exactly **one** `:focus` rule in 391 lines (`.skip-link:focus`, line 62) against **14** `:hover` rules — every button's designed state is mouse-only, while DESIGN.md twice promises to author focus. Separately, `read.html:62` carries `aria-hidden="true"` while all 9 of its controls stay tabbable and `visibility: visible` — focusable content inside an aria-hidden subtree is an outright ARIA violation. No `role="dialog"` anywhere; Escape does not close it.
*Fix:* add `:focus-visible` reusing the hover language (`box-shadow: 4px 4px 0 var(--ink)` + 2px vermilion outline); give the drawer `role="dialog" aria-modal="true"`, `inert` when closed, Escape-to-close, and focus return to `[data-vocab-open]`.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] Audio auto-plays with no mute and fails silently with no voice.**
`app.js:93` speaks unconditionally on every word tap. `app.js:29-44` returns false when no German voice exists, and that return is unchecked at **all 12 call sites**. `reader.js:262` renders an option literally labelled "System German voice" that may not exist.
*Why it matters:* an adult studying at 10pm gets talked at on their first tap, and the only volume control lives inside read.html's collapsed `<details>` — unreachable from cards.html and game.html, which also auto-speak.
*Fix:* a persistent speak-on-tap toggle on all four interactive pages; when `speak()` returns false, disable the audio controls and state "No German voice installed on this device."
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] The eyebrow-chip pattern on all six pages, and the 10px micro token.**
`hero-eyebrow-chip` ×8, `kicker-above-heading` ×38, `undersized-ui-text` ×236 — three rules, two root causes. Every page opens with `div.eyebrow` above its h1; `--type-micro` is 10px and carries "NEUES WORT" plus the level stamp on all 90 segments.
*Fix:* keep the eyebrow where it carries real information (`A1–B1 · 30 chapters`) and cut it where it is decoration (`Focused practice`, `Recognition game`, `Open source`, `Choose a path`). Raise `--type-micro` to 11–12px and re-tighten tracking.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable typeset`

**[P2] "Again" doesn't bring the card back; "Still learning" goes nowhere; the hero number is clipped.**
`cards.js:61-66` — pressing Again advances to a *different* word and only increments a counter. `game.js:40` says "Added to your learning path" while `learning` exists solely as a CSS class; nothing is persisted. `styles.css:19/119` — `.poster-number` overflows 90px and is clipped by `overflow: hidden`. Both `cards.js:30` and `game.js:11` shuffle with `sort(() => Math.random() - .5)`, which is biased and does not produce uniform permutations.
*Fix:* splice missed entries back into `deck` ~5 cards ahead; persist a `learning` set in store.js or change the copy to what actually happens; clamp `--type-poster` to the poster's real column (`clamp(5rem, 12vw, 9rem)` or a container query); swap in Fisher–Yates.
*Suggested command:* `/impeccable polish`
